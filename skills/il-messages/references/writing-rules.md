# Writing rules (the InvestorLists method)

These rules replace templates. The agent never shows a template or fills one in; it writes each message for this founder, this investor, this path.

## The pitch message is the core

- One short text that introduces the company and the raise. It is the founder's first LinkedIn message, the body of the cold email, and the "about us" inside every intro request. Everything else is a variant of it.
- Built from the raise profile. Shown, edited, saved as `message-set`. Rewritten only when the founder asks or when the raise profile changes.

## The five things investors look for at first glance

Founding team, market opportunity (why now), traction, moats and competitive advantages, existing investors and capital raised to date. Lead with the strongest one or two. Make the case on all five only when all five are strong. On LinkedIn the team line can be short, since the founder's profile headline carries it.

## The five questions that build the pitch message (answered in as little text as possible)

1. Why now: the opportunity in one sentence (market size, timing, regulation, new need).
2. Why you: founder-market fit, past successes.
3. Traction: the most noteworthy achievements (development, sales, contracts, partnerships).
4. Moats: patents, proprietary IP, key partnerships, licences or approvals, first to market.
5. Raised to date: amount, from whom (angels, VCs, grants), up to three notable names.

Shape: greeting with first name; one line on why this investor (sector experience, a portfolio company, a recent fund); two or three lines on the company with the strongest proof points; current investors if notable; one small ask ("would you be interested in more information?", "worth a quick call?"). Never two asks.

## Channel rules

LinkedIn (the method's preferred cold channel):
- Blank connection request, no note. On accept: the pitch message.
- 3rd degree: no request; InMail with the pitch message.
- Follow-up 1 at +4 days: short, offers to send information or get on a call.
- Follow-up 2 at +14 days, last: a recent update (partnership, patent, money raised) and an offer of a deck or a call; or a polite last-time close.
- Under 100 words for the first message, under 50 for follow-ups.

Cold email (more personalization than LinkedIn, since they do not see the profile):
- Subject names the market or the opportunity; preview text introduces the investor and the company.
- First line personalized: their investment in a similar niche, their recent fund, a portfolio company near the founder's space. From the record, the news or the brief; never invented.
- Second paragraph: the pitch message with a link to a short deck.
- Third: one ask (a quick call), booking link, "when would be a good time?"
- Signature with website and LinkedIn profile.
- Follow-up 1 at +2 days in the same thread: "Following up. Here is our introductory deck." Call ask, booking link.
- Follow-up 2 at +5 days in a new thread: "Not sure if you received my email last week. Sending it again here just in case:" then the first email.
- If connected on LinkedIn, a one-line LinkedIn note after each email: "Hi, did you get my email? I just emailed you again, thanks."

Email after a LinkedIn connection (the investor shared an email in the chat):
- Subject: "<Investor name> and <Company>: following our LinkedIn correspondence".
- Thanks for connecting; optional personalized line; a variant of the pitch message with the deck link; call ask with booking link; full signature. Send within the hour. Then on LinkedIn: "Thanks, I just emailed you from <address>."

Intro request to a connector (sent after the connector agreed in principle):
- Subject: "Introducing <Company> to <Investor name> from <Firm>".
- "Could you introduce me to <name> from <firm>?" One line of reason (stage of the raise, a portfolio company in the same sector). "About us:" a brief pitch variant. Deck link. Thanks, full signature. Written so the connector can forward it as is.

Reply to a warm intro:
- "<Introducer>, thank you for the kind introduction (moving you to bcc). <Investor>, great to connect. I'd love to schedule a 20-minute call to tell you more about <Company>. When would be a good time? If it's easier, you can book a time on my calendar." Update the introducer after the first meeting.

Submission form: the pitch message split into the form's fields, nothing the form does not ask for.

## Replies

- Positive interest ("sure", "sounds interesting"): "Great, what is your email? I'll send you our deck. Should we go over it in a brief call? When would be a good time? If it's easier, here is my calendar: <link>."
- Asks for a call: "That would be great. When would be a good time? If it's easier: <link>."
- Shares an email: email them within the hour (template above).
- "Not a good time": "Sure, when should I follow up with you on this?" On an answer, save the revisit date and reply: "Thanks, I'll reach out then. Is there anyone you recommend I talk to in the meantime?"
- "No, thank you": "Ok, thanks for your reply. Is there anyone else you think I should be in touch with?"
- "Thanks", a thumbs up, "great to meet you": no reply needed; send the next message in the sequence at its time.
- After the first meeting: email the same day, next step named, next meeting proposed; the first call is 30 minutes, no slides needed, ends with the next meeting booked.

## Always

- One hook per message, sourced from the record, the news or the brief; the source stays out of the message.
- No claim the record or the brief does not support. No invented familiarity ("great to see you at" only when the brief says they were there).
- No flattery, no "I hope this finds you well", no data dump: three proof points at most, the rest when they ask.
- Booking link, deck link and signature come from the raise profile; ask once if missing and save.
- Never two messages to the same person on the same hook. Never the same connector asked twice in a week.
- Plain words, short sentences, American English. No em dashes.
